# Day 14 — Corporate/Group Booking Outreach List + Pitch Template (Deep Dive Trips)

Goal: build a pipeline of corporate and large-group bookings — team outings, offsites, college
fests, bachelor(ette)/destination-wedding groups. This segment is worth chasing specifically
because it fills weekday/off-peak capacity that individual walk-in divers rarely book, it books in
blocks of 8-40+ people per event instead of 1-2, and a good first outing tends to become an annual
repeat (same HR team, next year's offsite) — the same compounding logic as Day 9's partner
referrals, but on the demand side instead of the referral side.

## Corporate/group segment framework

Ranked by expected deal size × ease of getting a yes. "How to find real prospects" below uses
searches that can actually be run today — not placeholders.

| # | Segment | Why it converts | How to find real prospects | Typical deal shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Corporate offsite/team-outing planners (event management companies that specialize in corporate offsites, not the corporates themselves) | They already have a roster of corporate clients asking for "something adventurous but safe," and they book venues/activities on behalf of many companies at once — one relationship can produce repeat bookings from multiple different corporates. | Google Maps / JustDial search `corporate team outing planners [city/region]` and `corporate offsite management company [city]`; also search LinkedIn for job titles "Event Manager" + "corporate offsites" based in the relevant city; check who exhibits at India's MICE trade events (OTM — Outbound Travel Mart, SATTE) for adventure/activity vendor listings. | Fixed per-head activity rate they mark up in their own package quote — give them a simple rate card by group-size tier so they can quote instantly without calling every time; a 10% referral fee is an alternative if they prefer commission over markup. |
| 2 | Corporate HR / L&D (Learning & Development) teams directly, at mid-size companies within day-trip distance of the dive site | HR teams that own the annual offsite budget will book direct (skipping the planner's markup) if approached with a ready-made "team outing package," especially for companies with 30-150 employees where a planner isn't already involved. | LinkedIn search `HR Manager` OR `People Operations` + city/region, filtered to companies with offices in day-trip range of the coast; also target IT parks / SEZ directories for the relevant city (each IT park usually publishes a tenant list) as a prospect source. | Package deal: welcome briefing + trial dive/snorkel session + team-building framing (trust exercises built around the buddy-check system), priced per head with a minimum group size (e.g. 15-20) to make a weekday slot worth blocking out. |
| 3 | College/university adventure clubs and fest organizing committees | College adventure/trekking clubs run annual trips and fests (technical fests, sports fests) with dedicated budgets and committees that re-form every year — a good relationship becomes an annual recurring booking even as the specific students change. | Instagram/LinkedIn search `[city] college adventure club` and `[city] university trekking club`; most fests have a public Instagram handle for their "adventure" or "outdoor" vertical — search `#[collegename]fest` during fest season (roughly Sept-Feb for most Indian campuses); NSS (National Service Scheme) and NCC units at nearby colleges also run outdoor activity days. | Discounted student group rate (bulk headcount, price-sensitive), paid upfront by the committee — good volume/low-margin category, useful for filling weekday off-season slots. |
| 4 | Bachelor(ette) party and destination-wedding planners | Group sizes of 8-20 for a bachelor(ette) trip or a full wedding party wanting one adventurous "day activity" between wedding events; planners handle multiple such groups per wedding season and will repeat the referral once one group has a good experience. | Instagram search `#destinationwedding[cityname]` and `#bachelorettetrip[region]`; wedding planner directories (WedMeGood, WeddingWire India) filtered to planners operating in the relevant coastal region — these list planner names and contact details directly. | Semi-private group session (not mixed with walk-in divers) at a per-head group rate, positioned as an "add a day of adventure" upsell the planner can slot into their itinerary pitch. |
| 5 | CSR / employee-engagement and team-building consultancies (marine conservation angle) | A beach cleanup + trial dive/snorkel combo is an easy CSR "employee engagement + give-back" package for companies with sustainability targets — this is a distinct pitch from category 1/2 because the buyer is the CSR/sustainability team, not HR. | LinkedIn search `CSR Manager` OR `Sustainability` + city; also search for marine conservation NGOs operating on the same coast (they often already run beach cleanups and may want a dive-operator partner for a joint CSR event pitch to corporates). | Fixed-price packaged CSR day (cleanup + briefing + trial dive/snorkel for a subset of attendees), invoiced with CSR-reportable documentation (photos, headcount, impact summary) since sustainability teams need this for internal reporting. |

## Corporate-readiness checklist (do this before the first pitch goes out)

Corporates and event planners will ask for these before they book or before finance approves
payment — none of this is optional once a real deal is on the table, so it's worth confirming now
rather than losing a deal to a slow "let me check" mid-negotiation:

- [ ] GST-compliant invoicing set up (most corporates cannot pay against a non-GST invoice)
- [ ] A one-page safety/certification summary (certifying body affiliation, instructor
      certifications, safety-diver ratio, insurance coverage) — HR and event planners will forward
      this to their own internal safety/legal sign-off before approving a booking
- [ ] A written group-size capacity limit per session/boat, so quotes never oversell what can
      actually be run safely in one slot
- [ ] A simple PDF one-pager (not a slide deck) with the group-rate card by headcount tier,
      what's included, and a booking-lead-time requirement (corporates typically plan offsites
      4-8 weeks out, so "book 3 weeks ahead minimum" is worth stating up front)
- [ ] Confirmation of whether purchase-order-based billing (invoice after the event, net-15/30)
      is workable, since many corporates don't pay upfront by personal card the way individual
      customers do

## Outreach sequence (do in this order once real details exist)

1. **Corporate offsite/event planners (category 1)** first — one relationship can produce several
   corporate bookings, and planners are used to being pitched by vendors so the cold-outreach
   conversion rate is typically higher than going straight to HR teams.
2. **College adventure clubs / fest committees (category 3)** in parallel — low friction, low
   deal size but useful for filling weekday off-season capacity while the higher-value categories
   are still being negotiated, and fest season has a hard calendar deadline worth hitting early.
3. **HR/L&D teams direct (category 2)** at 3-5 mid-size companies in day-trip range — worth the
   extra effort of a LinkedIn connection + short call rather than cold email alone, since this is
   the highest per-deal value category.
4. **Bachelor(ette)/wedding planners (category 4)** once wedding season for the region is known
   (check local wedding-season calendar once the coast/state is confirmed via business-info.md).
5. **CSR consultancies (category 5)** last — smallest addressable list, worth one outreach batch
   once the core categories are running and there's a real "we've hosted X groups safely" line to
   use as social proof.

Target: 10-12 prospect conversations in the first outreach round across categories 1-3, aiming
for 2-3 booked events in the first quarter — group/corporate sales cycles run longer than
individual bookings (weeks, not days), so this round is about pipeline-building, not instant
bookings.
